Hello and welcome to my personal website!

I am a Senior Lecturer in Machine Learning in the Department of Informatics at King's College London, where I head the Knowledge Representation, Reasoning and Multi-Agent Systems research group and am very fortunate to lead the MAASAI research lab (not the Maasai). I previously co-led the Economics and Finance Hub together with Bart de Keijzer and Carmine Ventre, and I am a member of the King's Institute for Artificial Intelligence and an elected member of King's Academic Board.

My research is on artificial intelligence and game theory, particularly multi-agent learning, reinforcement learning and the strategic behaviour of intelligent systems, with applications in financial markets, decentralised finance and cryptoeconomics.

Before coming to London in July 2022, I spent four very memorable years as a research fellow at the Singapore University of Technology and Design, hosted by Georgios Piliouras. I earned my PhD in game theory from the mathematics department of the National and Kapodistrian University of Athens, where I was very fortunate to be advised by Costis Melolidakis. I also hold an MSc in Statistics and Operations Research from the same university, a Diploma in Mathematics from TU Darmstadt, and a BSc (Hons) in Business Information Systems from the Athens University of Economics and Business.

For my full list of publications, please see my [CV] or [Google Scholar].

PhD positions
There are currently no funded PhD positions available. However, if you are interested in research in game theory, multi-agent systems, learning in games, reinforcement learning, AI for finance and/or cryptoeconomics (mechanism design and incentives), write to me about externally or self-funded PhD opportunities. Two projects are currently advertised through the department: Multi-Agent Learning with Large Language Models and AI and Machine Learning for Financial Markets. See the department's research degrees page for how to apply. When you write, please attach your CV and transcripts and tell me, in a few lines, which problem (or paper of mine) caught your interest and why.
