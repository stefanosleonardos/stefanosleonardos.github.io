I am a Senior Lecturer in Machine Learning in the Department of Informatics at King's College London, where I direct the MAASAI Lab and lead the Finance Hub.

My research sits between game theory and machine learning. On the theory side I study learning dynamics in multi-agent systems: when they converge, when they descend into chaos, and what equilibrium selection looks like in practice. On the applied side I use those tools to study digital markets — transaction fee mechanisms, block building auctions, market making and liquidity provision — where strategic agents interact at scale and the mechanism itself is up for design.

Before King's I spent four years as a research fellow at the Singapore University of Technology and Design, working with Georgios Piliouras. My PhD is in game theory, from the National and Kapodistrian University of Athens, advised by Costis Melolidakis; before that I read mathematics at TU Darmstadt.

Research

Multi-agent learning
Convergence, chaos and equilibrium selection in learning dynamics; policy gradient methods in Markov potential games; no-regret learning beyond worst-case bounds.
LLM agents
Strategic behaviour of language-model agents in interactive settings, curriculum design for LLM-based multi-agent systems, and what game theory can say about aligning them.
Market & mechanism design
Transaction fee mechanisms, block building auctions, maximal extractable value, and the centralisation pressures that emerge from ostensibly open market infrastructure.
Financial markets
Reinforcement learning for market making and liquidity provision, cryptocurrency price dynamics, and the systemic behaviour of learning agents in financial systems.
